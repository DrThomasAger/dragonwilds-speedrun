# Bayesian shrinkage: your data vs the reference runs

_Generated by `scripts/analysis/docs.mjs` from `data/kb/analysis/*.json` (2026-11-03). Runpath: `D:\labyrinth\Github\JaGeX\dragonwilds-speedrun\data\kb\runpath.json`._

## Plain English

With five attempts at a split, your average is noisy. With five hundred, it is your pace. Shrinkage blends the two automatically. Your estimate starts at the record holders' calibrated pace, as if it were **one** pseudo-attempt, and moves to your own data as fast as the data arrives.

## Split pace: Normal–Inverse-Gamma

On the log scale, `y_i = log(t_i − c_k)` is normal with unknown `(mu, sigma²)`. The conjugate prior is:

```
mu | sigma² ~ Normal(m0, sigma² / kappa0)
sigma²     ~ Scaled-Inv-chi²(nu0, s0²)
m0, s0     = mean of the calibrated TexasPete and Nanobit (mu_k, sigma_k)
kappa0 = 1   nu0 = 3
```

Posterior after `n` samples with mean `ybar` and sum of squared deviations `SS`:

```
kappa_n = kappa0 + n
mu_n    = (kappa0·m0 + n·ybar) / kappa_n            ← weight on your data = n / (kappa0 + n)
nu_n    = nu0 + n
s_n²    = (nu0·s0² + SS + kappa0·n/kappa_n·(ybar − m0)²) / nu_n
```

The simulator draws 2,048 `(mu, sigma)` pairs from this posterior and picks one per simulated run. Uncertainty about your pace therefore widens the predicted spread instead of being ignored.

### Skip misses are separated first (EM)

Your recorded split times include misses: a missed Keep hole costs ~44.26 s. Fitting a log-normal straight to them would mix "slow movement" with "missed a skip". So before shrinkage, each split's samples are fit as a **mixture**. Component `c` is a set of missed skips, with weight `prod p_j (made) × (1 − p_j)(1 − fatal_j) (missed)` and shift `L_c = sum of losses`. EM estimates the shared `(mu, sigma)` of `log(t − c_k − L_c)`:

```
E-step: r_ic ∝ w_c · LogNormal(t_i − c_k − L_c | mu, sigma)
M-step: mu = Σ r_ic·log x_ic / Σ r_ic,   sigma² = Σ r_ic·(log x_ic − mu)² / Σ r_ic
```

Then `(n, mu_EM, n·sigma_EM²)` go into the conjugate update as sufficient statistics.

## Skip success: Beta–Binomial

```
p_j ~ Beta(1 + made, 1 + missed)        mean (1 + made)/(2 + attempts)
```

This is Laplace's rule. After 0/0 it gives 50%; after 9/10 it gives 83%; after 90/100 it gives 89%. The practice priorities report how many clean reps in a row would move the mean to a target `p*`: `x = (p*(a + b) − a)/(1 − p*)`.

## Using your own data

Export the app's `dw_lab_v2` JSON (the app's export button). Raw `{timer, segPractice, practice}` or `{"dw_lab_v2": …}` both work. Then run:

```
node scripts/analysis/run-all.mjs --user path/to/export.json
```

`timer.attempts[].splits` (cumulative, null after a reset) and `segPractice[k][].t` become split samples. `practice[skipId].log[].ok` becomes the Beta counts. `practice[skipId].lost` overrides the miss cost.
